How to read a long report without losing your place

By the editors · 6 min read

Most of us skim. We open a long report, read the first two paragraphs with real attention, and then our eyes start jumping between headings, looking for the part that matters. By page four we have lost the thread, and we go back to the top to find it again.

Listening changes that. When a steady voice carries you through the text, your attention has something to hold on to. You stop rereading the same line. You notice the small qualifications that skimming misses, and you finish the document in one pass instead of three.

The trick is to keep your eyes on the page while you listen. Seeing each word as it is spoken ties the sound to the text, so you can glance at a chart or a table and come straight back to where you were. It also makes it easy to speed up. Most people can follow speech well past twice normal speed once they get used to it.

Start slow. Try one and a half times normal speed for a week, then nudge it up. Pause when something needs a second look, skip a paragraph you already know, and jump back a sentence when your mind wanders. Reading this way is less tiring, and you may find you remember more.

None of this needs a special app or a cleaned-up reading view. The page you already have open is enough.
